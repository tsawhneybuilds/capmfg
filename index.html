Thesis walkthrough

Credit can build factories, or merely inflate balance sheets.

The thesis asks whether production-oriented finance builds the supplier networks needed for durable manufacturing growth. New tests on real Indian plant data find a robust age-size gradient, no robust evidence that shifts toward imported inputs reduce later indigenous-input breadth, and persistent plant-level input losses. These are descriptive findings, not the thesis's final causal estimates.

The clean answer One hypothesis receives descriptive support: older plants are larger. The tested import-erosion relationship does not. No causal claim about bank credit, supplier destruction, or regional hysteresis is yet proved.

What The Real Data Say

Descriptive support

Older plants are larger

0.126 elasticity

Within the same two-digit industry and year, older plants have more employment. The estimate implies a 17.0% age-size difference between age 20 and age 5.

N = 624,067 plant-years; 217,711 identifier clusters; clustered SE = 0.0043; p < 0.001. The estimate remains 0.125 when restricted to repeated IDs with a stable reported production-start year.

Not supported

Import shifts do not predict supplier erosion

No robust negative association

A ten-point rise in directly imported input share predicts 0.012 additional indigenous input items next year, the opposite of erosion and economically tiny. The relationship becomes statistically null after trimming the largest 1% of import-share changes.

First differences with two-digit-industry-by-year effects. N = 247,096; 60,564 clusters. Trimmed estimate = 0.055 per full-share change, p = 0.344. Importer entry estimate = 0.002 items, p = 0.912.

Diagnostic

Input-breadth losses often persist

67.0% unrecovered

Among 12,059 plant episodes losing at least two major indigenous input codes, two-thirds had not regained the prior breadth within two subsequent observed years.

The unrecovered share ranges from 57.9% to 71.5% when the loss threshold moves from one to three items. This is a plant-level persistence diagnostic, not a regional hysteresis estimate.

Panel warning

Entry and exit are still blocked

74.6% contain gaps

Two-digit industry is stable across 96.3% of consecutive observations within a coding regime, but most repeated IDs disappear and later return. Only 38.1% report a perfectly stable production-start year.

The evidence supports panel feasibility, not clean births or deaths. Sampling rotation, temporary absence, and identifier repair remain unresolved.

How The Tests Work

Scale-up

Regress log employment on log one plus plant age, absorbing two-digit-industry-by-year effects and clustering standard errors by plant identifier. Re-estimate on stable-start-year IDs and after trimming the top and bottom 1% of employment.

Foreign sourcing

Measure directly imported share from ASI Block I and indigenous input breadth from major Block H items. In first differences, ask whether an import-share change predicts the following year's breadth change.

Persistence

Identify losses of one, two, or three major indigenous input codes and calculate whether the plant regains its pre-loss breadth within one or two subsequent consecutive observations.

Data boundary

Use 100 ASI source files from Blocks A, B, E, H, and I. Exclude 42 duplicated identifier-years and inconsistent aggregate totals. Keep comparison windows inside the same input-schedule regime. Do not use unvalidated survey weights or export plant identifiers.

What fixed effects remove

Industry-year effects absorb shocks common to plants in the same broad industry and year. First differences remove stable plant attributes in the import test.

What they do not remove

Import choices, survival, sampling, temporary absence, and reporting changes remain endogenous. The estimates therefore describe patterns; they do not identify policy effects.

What Work Was Done

1. Thesis design

Framed the supplier-network question around production credit, non-production credit, domestic processed inputs, foreign sourcing, and manufacturing hysteresis.

2. Measurement plan

Specified detailed processed-input breadth, supplier-demand exposure, production-bank exposure, and foreign-sourcing exposure.

3. India evidence machine

Built an ASI-focused pipeline for selection, scale-up, catch-up, productivity allocation, and formal-informal reallocation.

4. Data audit

Inventoried real ASI source blocks and identified the gates: state matching, identifier stability, deflators, weights, and missing-value rules.

5. Dashboard prototype

Made a public-facing dashboard structure with a persistent warning that all displayed numbers are synthetic placeholders.

6. Replication checks

Added tests for schema validation, decomposition identities, panel-linkage gating, reproducible synthetic data, and safe dashboard exports.

What Was Found

Positive finding

The age-size gradient is positive, precisely estimated, and stable after restricting the sample to repeated identifiers with a consistent reported production-start year and after trimming employment outliers.

Null and adverse evidence

Import-share increases do not robustly predict a contraction in later indigenous-input breadth. Identifier gaps and unstable start years still prevent credible entry, exit, and regional causal estimates.

The evidence has moved beyond a synthetic prototype, but only to provisional descriptive findings. The thesis's bank-credit mechanism and regional supplier-hysteresis claim still require a separate causal design.

What Is Supported, Not Supported, Open

Descriptively supported Older plants are larger within industry-year cells. This is robust to the main age and employment data-quality restrictions.
Not supported here Import shifts do not robustly predict a later loss of major indigenous input breadth. This is not proof that the true causal effect is zero.
Still open Production credit, non-production credit, regional supplier erosion, regional hysteresis, entry and exit, catch-up, and formal-informal reallocation.
